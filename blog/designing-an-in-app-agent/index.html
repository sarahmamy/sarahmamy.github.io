Designing an in-app agent

The navigation patterns I borrowed from Shopify, Notion and Amplitude: where the agent lives, panel versus full page, context, names, conversation lists, URLs and sharing.

Sarah MamyOctober 2026Mostly clickable

At Bigblue we're building an agent inside the app. The model turned out to be the easy part. The hard part was navigation. Where does it go in the navbar? Should it be a full page, or a panel you keep next to the order you're looking at? Can it be both? How do you open it from a page without losing that page? What do you call it, how do you list past conversations, do they deserve a URL, can you share one?

Rather than guess, I went looking at three products I use every day and admire for their design: Shopify, Notion and Amplitude. Big companies, strong product and design teams, and I genuinely have an account in each. What follows is what I found, as small mockups you can poke at rather than screenshots. Everything was observed in October 2026. These teams ship fast, so parts of this will age.

08What I'm keeping

  1. Don't file the agent under the nav. Put it where the work is: a composer or a button on every page, and a line at the bottom of search.
  2. Ship the panel and the full page from day one, with a toggle in the panel header. Let the panel survive navigation.
  3. Make the current page the default context, and show it as a chip people can remove.
  4. Conversations are content. List them by recency, let people rename and pin them, mark unread when an answer landed while they were away.
  5. Give every conversation a URL. Shopify's lack of one is the thing I missed most while writing this.
  6. Copy link is the floor. Permissions like any other document, and a share to Slack, are the ceiling.
